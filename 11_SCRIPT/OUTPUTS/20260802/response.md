# PART 1 — TITLE

Beyond Your Scarcity: Finding Abundance in His Presence

# PART 2 — SCRIPTURE

**Reading I: Jeremiah 55:1-13**
"Come, listen to me; and your soul shall live by my words; yes, my words shall fulfill what is right for eternity and for salvation."

**Reading II: Romans 8:37-39**
"But in all these things we are more than conquerors through him who loved us. For I am convinced that neither death nor life, nor angels nor principalities, nor powers, nor things present, nor things to come, nor height, nor depth, nor any other creature, in nothing will be able to separate us from the love of God in Christ Jesus our Lord."

**Gospel: Matthew 14:13-21**
"When Jesus heard it, he withdrew to a deserted place by himself to pray. But when the crowds heard of it, they came to him from the cities and followed him. When he saw the great crowds, he had compassion for them, and he healed their sick. As evening approached, the disciples came to him, saying, 'Dismiss the crowds, so that they may go to the villages and buy food for themselves.' But Jesus said to them, 'They need not go away. You give them food.' They said, 'We have here only five loaves and two fish.' He said, 'Bring them here to me.'... They all ate and were satisfied, and the fragments that were left were gathered up, twelve baskets full, which were collected by those who gathered them."

# PART 3 — THEME

When we face the vastness of our needs and the emptiness of our resources, we are invited to move from a mindset of scarcity to a life of divine abundance. Through the compassion of Christ, our meager offerings—when surrendered to Him—become instruments of His miraculous grace that satisfy the soul and overflow for the world.

# PART 4 — HISTORICAL BACKGROUND

In the first century, the "deserted place" mentioned in the Gospel likely refers to the rugged, limestone terrain east of the Sea of Galilee. For a crowd gathered for healing and teaching, the setting was physically taxing. The "five loaves and two fish" refers to barley loaves—the staple food of the poor—and a few small fish, likely dried or salted to preserve them. In the economy of the ancient Near East, such a meal would barely sustain a single family. The "twelve baskets" remaining signify not just leftovers, but an abundance that reflects the twelve tribes of Israel, signaling that God’s provision is meant for all His people.

# PART 5 — SCRIPTURE MEDITATION

(Imagine a soft, cinematic swell of music—perhaps a cello or a gentle ambient pad. The visual is a wide shot of the sun beginning to dip behind the hills of Galilee, casting long, golden shadows over a restless, hungry crowd.)

The world often speaks to us in the language of "not enough." 

We look at our bank accounts and see a void. We look at our energy and see exhaustion. We look at our capacity to love, to forgive, or to endure suffering, and we see a desert—dry, cracked, and incapable of sustaining life. We feel like the disciples standing on that shoreline, looking at the vastness of the crowd's need and comparing it to the smallness of our own hands. "Dismiss them," we whisper. "They have nothing but what we have, and it is not enough."

But listen to the voice of the Prophet Jeremiah. God cries out to a wandering soul: *"Come, listen to me; and your soul shall live by my words."*

God does not offer a temporary fix; He offers His own Word—a reality that sustains us through eternity. 

(The scene shifts: We see Jesus. He is not looking at the crowd with frustration, but with *compassion*. The Greek word used is *splagchnizomai*—a deep, visceral movement of the heart.)

Observe the movement of the miracle. It begins not with a multiplication of bread, but with an act of surrender. "Bring them here to me." 

Jesus takes the inadequate. He takes the insufficient. He takes the small, the broken, and the meager, and He lifts His eyes to the Father. In His hands, the math of heaven is different than the math of earth. In His hands, scarcity dies, and abundance is born. 

Perhaps you are standing on that shoreline today. You feel inadequate for your vocation, too broken to serve, too tired to pray. You feel that your life is merely "five loaves and two fish"—small, common, and insufficient for the hunger of this world. 

Do not dismiss your scarcity. Bring it to Him. 

Surrender your "not enough" to the One who is "All in All." For when you place your life in His hands, you discover a truth that no shadow can obscure, no trial can diminish, and no depth can swallow: that in Christ Jesus, you are more than a conqueror, and His love is the only thing in all creation that can never, ever be taken from you.

# PART 6 — LIFE APPLICATION

1. **Surrender the Small Things:** Instead of focusing on how little you have to offer, offer what you *do* have—your time, your small talent, your brief prayer—to Jesus, and ask Him to sanctify it.
2. **Counter the Scarcity Mindset:** When anxiety tells you there is "not enough" (time, love, peace), remind yourself of Romans 8: the sufficiency of God's love.
3. **Respond to Compassion:** Look for the "crowds" in your life—the people around you who are hungry for hope or healing—and realize that God wants to work through your small acts of kindness.
4. **Rest in the Promise:** When life feels overwhelming, return to the Word of God. Let His promises be the foundation that sustains your soul when your own strength fails.

# PART 7 — PRAYER

Lord Jesus, I come to You today carrying my "not enough." I bring You my exhaustion, my doubts, and my meager resources. I often look at the needs of the world and my own weaknesses, and I feel overwhelmed. 

Forgive me for the times I try to solve everything in my own strength, forgetting that You are the source of all life. I place my life, my family, and my struggles into Your hands. Transform my scarcity into Your abundance. Teach me to trust that Your grace is sufficient for every need, and remind me daily that nothing can ever separate me from Your love. Amen.

# PART 8 — KEY TAKEAWAYS

*   God’s Word is the only nourishment that sustains the soul eternally.
*   Compassion is the bridge between human need and Divine miracle.
*   When we surrender our "little" to Christ, He makes it "more than enough."
*   Nothing in heaven or on earth can separate us from the love of God in Christ.
